"Science is not for girls." "I'm not the leader type." "People from my area don't go to university."

You may have heard sentences like these so often that they stopped sounding like opinions. They started to sound like facts. That is what a limiting belief is: an idea about yourself or the world that holds you back, which you accept without checking.

Limiting beliefs rarely come from nowhere. They come from things we hear at home, in school, in church or mosque, on social media and from friends. Some come from real experiences, such as failing a test or being laughed at. The problem is not that you heard them. The problem is when they decide your choices for you.

## How to spot a limiting belief

Listen for these signs in your own thoughts:

- **Absolute words.** "I always fail maths." "Nobody will listen to me."
- **Borrowed voices.** The thought sounds exactly like something a parent, teacher or friend once said.
- **Fear dressed as fact.** "There's no point applying" can really mean "I'm scared of being rejected."
- **Rules about who you are allowed to be.** "Girls like me don't..."

Write the thought down. Seeing it on paper makes it easier to question.

## Five ways to shift it

**1. Ask: is this a fact or a feeling?**
"I failed my last maths test" is a fact. "I am bad at maths" is a conclusion. One test does not decide who you are.

**2. Look for evidence on the other side.**
Have you ever solved a hard problem? Has anyone from your community done the thing you think is impossible? Nigerian women run hospitals, banks, labs, courtrooms and companies. Find one example and learn her story.

**3. Rewrite the sentence.**
Swap "I can't" for "I can't yet" or "I'm learning how." Swap "Girls don't do this" for "Not many girls do this yet, so I would be one of the first." Small changes in words change what feels possible.

**4. Choose your circle carefully.**
The people around you shape what you think you can do. Spend time with friends, teachers and mentors who take your goals seriously. If someone always tells you what you can't do, you don't have to treat their opinion as final.

**5. Take one small step.**
Belief often follows action, not the other way round. Ask one question in class. Fill one application. Attend one club meeting. Each small step gives you new evidence that the old belief was wrong.

## When the barrier is real

Not every barrier is in your head. School fees, distance, family duties, and unsafe roads are real. Shifting a belief will not pay a fee. But it can help you ask for help, look for a scholarship, or speak up about what you need. That is often where change starts.

## For parents, teachers and mentors

Girls learn what is possible from what we say and what we show. Notice when you praise boys for being bold and girls for being quiet. Give girls hard problems and leadership roles. Tell them about women who did what they want to do.

> A girl who believes the door is closed will not knock. Part of our work is helping her see which doors are open, and helping open the ones that are not.

*If something you read here brings up feelings that are hard to handle, talk to an adult you trust.*
