Many girls are raised to be polite, helpful and agreeable. Those are good qualities. But when "be nice" means never saying no, it becomes a problem. You end up doing things you don't want to do, giving away time you need, or putting up with treatment that hurts you.

A boundary is a line you draw to protect yourself. It tells other people what is okay with you and what is not.

## Types of boundaries

- **Physical.** Who can touch you, and how. Your body belongs to you.
- **Emotional.** How people speak to you, and what you are willing to share.
- **Time.** How much of your time goes to chores, friends, phone calls and favours, and how much is left for study and rest.
- **Digital.** What you post, who can message you, and whether you share passwords or photos.
- **Money and things.** What you lend, borrow or give away.

## How to set a boundary

**1. Know what matters to you.**
Your studies, your safety, your faith, your rest. When you know your priorities, it is easier to see when something crosses a line.

**2. Say it simply.**
You don't need a long speech. Try:
- "No, I can't do that."
- "Please don't touch me."
- "I don't share my password."
- "I'll help after I finish my homework."

**3. Don't over-explain.**
"No" is a full sentence. The more you justify, the more room people have to argue.

**4. Be consistent.**
If you say no today and yes tomorrow to the same thing, people learn that your no is negotiable.

**5. Expect some push-back.**
People who benefited from you having no boundaries may not like the change. That does not mean you are wrong.

## Respecting other people's boundaries

Boundaries work both ways. When a friend says no, accept it. When someone looks uncomfortable, stop and ask. Respecting other people's limits is part of being trustworthy.

## When a boundary is about your safety

Some boundaries are not about manners. They are about safety. If anyone, including a relative, teacher, neighbour, religious leader or boyfriend, touches you in a way that feels wrong, pressures you for sex, asks for nude photos, or threatens you, it is not your fault, and you do not have to keep it secret.

- **Tell an adult you trust** as soon as you can: a parent, aunt, teacher, school counsellor or mentor.
- **If they don't help, tell someone else.** Keep telling until someone acts.
- **Know that the law is on your side.** Nigeria's Child Rights Act (2003) protects children from abuse and exploitation, and the Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act (2015) makes rape, coercion and many other forms of violence crimes.
- **If you are in immediate danger,** go to a safe place and contact the police.

## For parents and carers

Teach children the correct names for their body parts and that no one has a right to touch them without permission. Let them say no to hugs they don't want, even from relatives. A child who is allowed to say no at home is better able to say no, and to tell you, when it really matters.
